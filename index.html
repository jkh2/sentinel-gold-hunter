How the score works

Four independent channels are built on one ground-distance grid, each normalized 0–1:

History: MRDS gold sites (producers count most) plus embedded historic districts.

Geochem: USGS stream-sediment, soil, and pan-concentrate samples. A sample only scores if it sits in the top ~12% for gold (or ~10% for arsenic, a gold pathfinder) among samples run by the same method. A cell only counts this channel where samples were actually taken.

Satellite: Sentinel-2 SWIR ratios for clay/sericite (B11/B12) and iron oxide (red/blue), on bare ground only, ranked against the rest of the scene, then filtered for contiguous patches.

Geology: coarse favorability of the major U.S. gold provinces.

The surface is the weighted average of channels that have data at each spot, boosted where two or more agree. Confidence rises with documented producers, independent agreement, and sampling density.

Honesty notes. This is a relative prospectivity model, not assay data or a promise of gold. MRDS stopped systematic updates in 2011, and most geochem sampling is decades old, so absence of a record is not absence of gold. Satellite alteration also lights up mine dumps, roads, and some bare soils; it sees the surface only, and forest hides it. Land status is advisory.
